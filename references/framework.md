# Roadmap, strategies and hierarchy

Use the operational synthesis below, derived from the three reference figures used to define this skill. The razor illustrates differentiated component routing; do not generalize its cartridge architecture or return arrangement to other products.

## Six strategy axes: design choices to test

| Axis | Question | Product-adapted evidence/measure |
|---|---|---|
| Narrow | Can fewer resources deliver the same service? | Virgin/total material per functional unit including replacements |
| Slow (long life) | Can it remain useful longer before replacement? | Reliable life/cycles, performance retention, obsolescence dependencies |
| Slow (life extension) | Can intervention or transfer extend useful life? | Defined failure repair coverage, access time, spare support, remaining life |
| Close | Can end-of-life materials return to production? | Actual collection, acceptance, yield, recovered quality and destination |
| Regenerate | Can resources/processes support safe renewal/restoration? | Traceable sourcing, chemistry, ecological outcomes and trade-offs |
| Inform | Can information improve use and next-use decisions? | Accuracy/accessibility, diagnosis/task performance, actor responsibility |

Screen all six; justify omissions. Their order is not a ranking. One choice can serve multiple axes; a roadmap step can use several axes. Do not force every axis into every step. For example, disassembly can support life extension and Close, but proves neither outcome by itself.

## Hierarchy: next action on an actual unit or component

| Priority | Route | Eligibility and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Maintain | Unit is fit for intended service with acceptable remaining life; keep it operating with appropriate preventive care. Use condition/service records; no intervention is required merely to claim maintenance. |
| 2 | Repair | A specific fault can be corrected safely and proportionately. Identify failure, restored performance, cost, spares and remaining life. |
| 3 | Reuse | A working used unit can be redeployed for the same intended purpose. Verify condition, compatibility, acceptance and demand. |
| 4 | Refurbish | Limited rework can restore an explicitly defined good condition. Specify work, acceptance grade and remaining service. |
| 5 | Remanufacture | Controlled rebuilding can restore the applicable original specification or defined as-new performance. Specify inspection, replacement, tests and accountable process. |
| 6 | Recycle | Higher-value routes are infeasible and materials can actually return to production. Verify separation, acceptance, contamination, yield, quality and destination. |

Use the order as a search preference, not six compulsory operations. Skip repair for a functioning returned unit; do not refurbish one already fit for reuse. Distinguish preventive maintenance from corrective repair. Maintaining an assembly does not imply that every consumed/worn fraction is reusable.

Record each higher-route exclusion as not applicable, evidenced infeasible, or unresolved, with reason. Consider essential performance, safety/hygiene, remaining life, demand, technical feasibility, costs and impacts. Missing evidence is not proof of infeasibility; retain conditional routes and validation tasks.

Distinguish **enabled by design**, **operationally feasible**, and **actually achieved**. Do not award arbitrary universal points to routes or average ordinal positions as a circularity percentage. Mass retention does not capture all function, processing effort or economic value. Record unavoidable disposal/loss separately; explicitly label any additional biological or repurposing route.

## Product-independent adaptation

- Choose a service-based functional unit: task count, operating hours at stated output, delivered quantity at defined quality, or another justified unit. Do not universally use a year or one purchase.
- For durable assemblies with consumables, allocate replacements and added infrastructure over the same service. For multiple lives, define the cohort/time boundary and avoid counting the same mass as reused and recycled in a single routing event.
- For chemicals, indivisible materials and consumed/short-life goods, justify inapplicable technical retention routes; investigate demand, delivery/packaging, safe recovery and biological cycles when appropriate.
- Include software/support/accessory dependencies where they constrain useful life, without turning the task into UI redesign.
- Use the razor only as an illustration: durable and worn parts may require different next actions. Do not prescribe handles, cartridges, subscriptions or take-back universally.
