# Deliverables

Match the package to the requested maturity. Do not create every artifact by default.

Apply the clear-writing rules in SKILL.md throughout. Use simple, short English sentences while retaining necessary technical vocabulary. Present conclusions first, with only the explanation needed to understand them. Prefer compact comparison tables and short concept descriptions. Place specific evidence/uncertainty labels beside the relevant result; avoid repeating broad disclaimers. Keep supporting calculations and traceability available without making the main explanation hard to scan.

## Roadmap-aligned reporting requirements

Organize a complete redesign around the user's Steps 0-6 in process.md. Include the roadmap application table linking each choice to strategy axes, component routes and verification measures; a six-axis concept coverage matrix; and a component routing register with evidence for higher-route exclusions. These are distinct outputs, not interchangeable tables.

Show assessment at Step 0, updates at Steps 1-5 and reassessment/iteration at Step 6. Keep physical circularity indicators primary; use design scores only as supplementary measures when defensible. In the package lists below, a request for a score means a supported score or an explicit unavailable result, not permission to invent a number. Quantitative comparison may use a clearly separate scenario; do not present it as measured product performance. If information cannot support a unique ranking, present conditional/tied or non-dominated options with validation tasks.

For greenfield design, substitute an explicit service-equivalent benchmark for an existing-product audit and clearly identify that distinction. Give the DPP and each strategy/route a reasoned applicability decision. Preserve the baseline and measurement requirements for any product; do not force a razor-like architecture.

When the user requests the complete process, asks to test the full skill, or gives no stopping depth after accepting the guided workflow, use the end-to-end conceptual package below as the default. A narrower explicit request still controls.

## Preliminary visual diagnostic

- product and use summary;
- annotated visible observations;
- evidence register and missing data;
- preliminary lifecycle and circularity hotspots;
- two to four next questions;
- candidate redesign directions with confidence limits.

## Baseline audit

- functional unit and system boundary;
- time horizon, use conditions, criteria, weights, rating rules, and data-quality rules;
- reference functions, use, performance, and current lifecycle map;
- functional architecture, components, bill of materials, masses, joints, and service logic at the available detail;
- maintenance, failure, obsolescence, repairability, life, disassembly, reuse, refurbishment, return, and end-of-use evidence;
- necessary loop actors, infrastructure, strengths, weaknesses, and missing data;
- a real-product assessment followed, if needed, by a distinctly named hypothetical reference scenario with central values, ranges, rationales, and validation actions;
- baseline circularity score and indicator panel without penalizing unknowns;
- sustainability hotspots and data-quality statement.

## Concept and selection package

Include a standard concept sheet and explicit feasibility matrix for every retained concept, following concepts-and-cad.md. Include each visual's consistency-check result and unresolved limitations. Record the selected concept ID, decision authority/status and any outstanding conditions before CAD development. For generated concepts eliminated at screening, preserve the reason for rejection without requiring full development.

- concept brief and user scenario;
- functional architecture and module tree;
- strategy-coverage matrix showing which concepts address which priority strategies;
- a consistent image or comparison-board panel for every retained concept before selection;
- material, joint, maintenance, disassembly, and return-loop strategy;
- requirements compliance matrix;
- baseline plus alternative concepts;
- quantitative score and physical indicators for the baseline and every retained concept using common criteria and weights;
- intended life; virgin material per functional unit including replacements; retained mass after a defined repair; disassembly time and operations; reuse and refurbishment potential; actual or hypothetical collection and recovery, when relevant;
- per-concept advantages, disadvantages, risks, gates, evidence confidence, and sensitivity analysis where needed;
- a refined final render after selection;
- selection rationale, risks, unresolved trade-offs, and validation plan.

## CAD-ready package

- CAD-ready handoff from `concepts-and-cad.md`;
- geometry files when a suitable CAD capability is available;
- export and dimensional checks;
- explicit maturity label: visual concept, parametric concept, engineering CAD, or production release candidate.

## End-to-end conceptual STEP package

- opening intake answers and evidence/assumption register;
- baseline diagnosis presented before concepts, plus functional unit, lifecycle boundary, time horizon, use conditions, and scoring rules;
- normally three to five structurally different concepts, adjusted to cover the relevant strategies, plus the unchanged baseline reference;
- strategy-coverage matrix and comparable visual panel for every retained concept;
- traceable multi-concept scoring output, physical indicators, advantages, disadvantages, risks, gates, and confidence;
- selection rationale and refined final conceptual visualization;
- frozen constructed scenario and physical indicators;
- CAD-ready brief with module tree, interfaces, datums, envelopes, disassembly, and unresolved parameters;
- parametric CAD source;
- conceptual STEP assembly and useful part STEP files;
- STEP verification record covering schema when detectable, units, solid/body count, round-trip import, and bounding box;
- final before/after report and explicit limitations.

If evidence is missing, assumptions may support this package. Label the output as a conceptual or nonfunctional model and keep safety-critical geometry unresolved or disabled.

## Final before/after report

Report the purpose, product, functional unit, boundary, evidence quality, baseline, goals, strategies, concepts, selection rationale, selected redesign, before/after scores, physical indicators, sustainability consequences, burden shifts, feasibility, risks, validation status, and conclusions limited to the evidence.

Use tables or charts when they materially clarify comparisons. Always label constructed scenarios, estimated data, and illustrative renders.

## Pre-selection completeness check

Apply this full-package check only to a full redesign selection. An explicitly requested preliminary, concise or text-only assessment takes precedence: label any provisional recommendation, show supported findings and uncertainties, and do not force images, scoring, CAD or STEP. The subsequent-development instruction below is conditional on the requested scope.

Do not select the final concept until the package actually presents all seven items:

1. baseline diagnosis;
2. quantified baseline or a clearly named hypothetical reference scenario;
3. distinct concepts and comparable illustrations;
4. measures for every retained concept;
5. a common comparison table;
6. ranking sensitivity, ties, and robustness statement;
7. selection rationale and remaining validation.

A future-work list does not satisfy a missing item. After this check, continue the existing workflow through selected-concept development, final visualization, CAD-ready handoff, and verified conceptual STEP when tools permit.
