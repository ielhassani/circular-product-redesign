# Roadmap implementation: Steps 0-6

Use the user's sequence for any product; read framework.md for independent strategy axes and component hierarchy. Carry stable concept, component, requirement, assumption and indicator IDs through all steps. Assess continuously.

## 0. If the product already exists: assess the current product

Define service, quality/performance, duty cycle, geography, time horizon and boundary, including replacements, consumables, maintenance, enabling equipment and next-use processing.

Present the baseline before ideation: functions; architecture; components; known materials, masses and joints; failures, service life, maintenance and disassembly; current business model; actual current routes, actors/infrastructure; strengths, hotspots and evidence gaps. Separate durable parts, wear parts, consumables and packaging.

Establish the evidence register and common measurement panel. Show supported baseline circularity indicators and limitations. If calculation requires assumptions, create a separately named hypothetical reference; never turn unknowns into zero repairability/recovery. For greenfield design, mark the current-product audit not applicable and select an explicit service-equivalent benchmark.

**Output/gate:** baseline diagnosis, current component routes, comparison definitions, initial indicators and testable improvement targets.

## 1. Define a circular business model

Screen all six design axes and component retention opportunities. Generate distinct concept families coupling architecture with how value is delivered and retained. Consider durable sale with service/spares, refill, shared access, leasing, resale, take-back or other appropriate arrangements; impose none universally.

Specify user, value proposition, ownership, who pays/benefits, maintenance obligations, spare/service capacity, use, incentives, responsible actors, next-use demand and plausible costs/revenues. Explain who finances retained value. Include added infrastructure in the boundary.

**Output/gate:** distinct concept families (normally three to five), six-axis coverage, intended routes, measurable requirements and explicit feasibility assumptions. Mark unsupported demand/operator economics conditional.

## 2. Design for disassembly (DfD)

Design selective diagnosis/access/separation for intended component routes. Match module boundaries to wear, failure, contamination and different service lives. Preserve intact functional assemblies where useful; maximum teardown is not the objective.

Specify joints, fasteners, tools, access, sequence, reassembly, compatibility, spare availability and condition checks. Measure time/operations to the target component, damage and retained mass for a defined intervention. Distinguish targets from timed tests.

Check relevant sealing, strength, hygiene, isolation and other essential requirements. For indivisible/consumed products, justify limited applicability and assess delivery equipment, packaging or separable fractions as appropriate.

**Output/gate:** architecture and service/disassembly plan linked to component routes, measures and unresolved validation.

## 3. Radical material simplification

Challenge unnecessary mass, material types, incompatible mixtures, coatings, additives and adhesives. Consider secondary inputs and safe renewable resources. Do not assume that lower mass, fewer parts, mono-materiality, recycled content or biobased origin demonstrates improvement.

Check durability, service access, barriers/function, scrap, contamination, critical materials, recovery quality and repeated cycling. Evaluate material per delivered service including replacements. Use Regenerate only with explicit ecological/process evidence; renewable origin alone remains a hypothesis.

**Output/gate:** material/mass budget, compatibility/separation plan and resource measures with lifetime/function trade-offs.

## 4. Use a digital product passport (DPP), where relevant

Identify actors and decisions needing information. Specify relevant identity/version, materials, hazards, interfaces, instructions, spare references, service history, condition tests and route information. Define data creators, verification, update responsibility, access and lifetime availability.

Choose physical markings, manuals, service records, database or DPP proportionate to need. Decide product, batch, component or material granularity. Verify jurisdiction, product group and date before claiming a legal requirement. A QR code is only an access mechanism.

Measure data accuracy/accessibility or improvement in diagnosis/routing/tasks; account for data-maintenance cost/burden.

**Output/gate:** minimum information specification and justified DPP/simpler-system/not-applicable decision.

## 5. Engineer a reverse supply chain

Specify continued use or collection, safe handling/storage, inspection/triage, intervention/processing, quality checks, redeployment/destination and residues. Identify ownership, incentives, capacities, distances, batch size, cost and acceptance conditions.

Apply the hierarchy by component and actual condition. Distinguish repair in place, broken returns, working returns and material residues. Verify actual operator acceptance and next-use demand. Do not invent providers, customers or rates; use conditional scenarios and verification tasks when unknown.

Track eligible population, collected fraction, accepted fraction, processing yield and output destination separately. Include delay, inventory, contamination, rejects and quality. A take-back promise does not demonstrate recovery; local maintenance/reuse may not require return transport.

**Output/gate:** component routing register, logistics/actors, quantity/mass flow balance, costs, acceptance evidence and fallback.

## 6. Evaluate and improve the redesign

Reassess baseline and every retained concept using Step 0 definitions. Report useful life, material demand/loss per functional unit, defined repair retention, disassembly and actual/scenario routing as applicable. Separate baseline, target and achieved outcomes. Calculate supplementary design scores only when supported and MCI only with its published method and required inputs.

In a full conceptual run, present comparable visuals plus per-concept advantages, disadvantages, indicator changes, routing evidence and uncertainty. Evaluate environmental, social, economic and engineering consequences separately. Test influential physical assumptions as well as any scoring weights; rating-only sensitivity is not physical-flow modelling.

Check mandatory requirements. Identify robust improvements, conditional preferences, ties or non-dominated trade-offs. Record rejected alternatives and higher-route exclusions. Do not force a numerical total ranking.

Develop the selected concept and requested/full-workflow visualization/CAD/STEP handoff. Define tests, acceptance criteria, owners and decisions they unlock. File integrity is not engineering validation.

**Output/gate:** comparison, selection rationale, validation status, limitations and iteration actions. Return serviceability failures to Step 2, incompatible materials to Step 3, missing information to Step 4, and weak return economics/demand to Steps 1/5; update affected indicators and conclusions.
